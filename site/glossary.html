Attention
The reading step before each guess: the model re-reads the whole conversation and weighs which parts matter right now. Like a good host glancing across the room before answering you. Long chats can leave old details faint.
Base model
The raw, pre–finishing-school model. Brilliant at continuing text, unreliable at answering you. A plain name with no “Talk/Chat/Instruct” suffix usually means this.
Benchmark
A fixed exam with known answers used to score models. A measurement, not a promise. (The companion project AI Benchmarks Explained covers reading those scores honestly.)
Chat / instruct model
The model after finishing school: answers, clarifies, declines gracefully. This is what every chat service serves and what you almost always want for everyday use.
Cutoff date
The last date of text the model trained on — the back page of its library. Everything after it is unknown to the model unless it's given a reading tool.
Fine-tune
An extra, narrower round of finishing school taught on top of a finished model — a specialty stacked on a standard base. Shows up in names as an extra tag.
Hallucination
A confident, well-formed answer for a fact the model never held: the guess-the-next-word game filling an empty slot with a plausible invention. A property of the game, not a lie — and not fully patchable.
Inference
The seconds clock: the model answering, guess by guess, after its training clock has stopped. No new learning sticks to the model during inference.
Local model
A model running on your own computer (e.g., through Ollama). It never quietly re-seasons itself; updates arrive as new downloads, which is why local users know their vintage precisely.
Parameter
One adjustable setting inside the model — a “dial” that training nudged a hair at a time until it held part of the model's feel for language. There are billions.
Pretraining
The reading phase (chapter one-and-a-half of every model's life): billions of guess-the-next-word rounds across a giant library.
Prompt
The words you send. Your most durable tool: label a good one with the vintage it worked on, and it survives every model change with minor adjustment.
Release
A trained model frozen into a finished, named version — the years clock, boxed and labeled for the shelf.
RLHF (human feedback)
The tasting panel: humans compare two replies and pick the better dish, and thousands of verdicts nudge the model's taste. Your thumbs-up is a tiny stream of the same signal, aimed at the next vintage.
System prompt
The hidden instructions that set a chat service's tone and rules. Part of the kitchen, not the cook — adjustable by the provider any week.
Temperature
The seasoning dial for word-choice adventure: low means nearly the top of the menu (starchy, predictable, good for facts); high means happily down-menu (lively, risky, good for poems).
Token
A word-chunk. Models read and write in tokens, which is why a model told “say a ten-letter word” counts clumsily — tokens aren't letters.
Training
The years clock: billions of guess-the-next-word rounds across a giant library of text, each miss nudging every parameter a hair.
Version / vintage
Which edition of the recipe you're talking to. A version bump means the entire schooling — reading, finishing school, tasting panel — was re-run over months, and the shelf gets a differently named jar.
