Finishing school vs. the tasting panel

The trained-from-scratch model knows how the words go but not the job. Two coaching phases fix that:

Phase 1 — Finishing school

What it is: thousands of ideal conversations, written by professional drafters and reviewers, used as worked examples.

Result: the model learns to answer rather than continue, to ask a clarifying question when needed, and to decline gracefully. (This is the difference between a “base” model and a “Talk/Chat/Instruct” model.)

Phase 2 — Tasting panel

What it is: humans compare two replies to the same question and pick the better dish — thousands of times.

Result: taste. Concise over padded, warm over snide, “I'm not certain” said once instead of hedging every clause. (In tech press, this starts with “RL…”)

Your thumbs-up and thumbs-down are a small stream of the same dish signal — fed to the next cohort of training, not back into the model you're chatting with tonight. The model you're talking to is frozen; the next vintage is months away.

The six habits

  1. Match the tool to the stakes: fluency jobs (drafting, rewording, brainstorming) are safe; fact jobs (citations, prices, dates, medical specifics) need checking.
  2. Ask for sources — then actually check them.
  3. Re-ask in a fresh chat: two wildly different answers means one is fictitious.
  4. Have it quote exactly rather than paraphrase.
  5. Ask “what would make this wrong?” — doubts surface quickly.
  6. For health, money, or law: a trusted human professional decides. Education, never advice.
