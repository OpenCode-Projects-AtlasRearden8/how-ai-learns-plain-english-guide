What each piece means

PiecePlain meaning
FamilyWhose kitchen built it, with whose training recipe. A personality label — not a quality score.
Size (7B)Seven billion adjustable settings (parameters). A size class: 1–3B phone-friendly, 4–9B everyday laptop, 10–40B strong machine, 70B+ serious hardware. B-numbers compare well only within one family, and size is not quality.
FlavorWhich finishing school: no suffix or “Base” means the raw, pre-school reader (may ramble at a chat window); Talk/Chat/Instruct means finished school; a Think/R-style variant works out answers on paper first; an extra tag is a specialty stacked on top.
VersionWhich vintage. A bump means the whole schooling was re-run — months of work that arrive as a new name, not a slowly improving same-model.

Update cadence: the two-season kitchen

Model behavior changes on two schedules at once:

What changesHow oftenWhat it means for you
New vintage (re-run training + coaching, new name) Roughly seasonal per family — a few months to a year between flagships A genuinely different model wearing a similar name. Skim the release notes once per cycle; re-run your own little trial drills after the big jumps.
The kitchen, re-seasoned (system prompt, reading tools, guardrails, serving settings) Quietly, any week, with no announcement Chats feel different while the model name stays put. This is normal — the frozen vintage is frozen; the seasoning program is not.

The gentle protocol (five habits)

  1. Write down your model's vintage when it matters: “Toy-v3-Talk, March.”
  2. Skim official release notes once per cycle — skip the rumor mill.
  3. Ask the model its training cutoff, then verify the answer against a document before pinning anything to it.
  4. Re-run two or three of your personal sanity questions after a big vintage jump.
  5. Don't chase minors. Sub-version drift is background noise for everyday work.

For local models (downloaded onto your own machine), there is no secret seasoning at all: the model never changes until you download a new file — one reason local-AI people know their vintage precisely. Your prompt library, meanwhile, is the most durable thing you own: label good prompts with the vintage they worked on, and they'll survive every change of cook with minor adjustment.
